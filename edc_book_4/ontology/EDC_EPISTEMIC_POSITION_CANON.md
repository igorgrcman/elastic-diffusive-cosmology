## Full Statement

Elastic-Diffusive Cosmology (EDC) is a 5D membrane-based research program
that seeks to explain observed 3D physics as a projected or reduced manifestation
of deeper geometric, topological, and dynamical structure. In that sense, EDC is
not merely a catalogue of numerical matches: its core ambition is to derive the
allowed structure of physical reality from higher-dimensional principles and only
then relate that structure to empirically measured observables.

At the present stage of development, however, different kinds of EDC results carry
different epistemic weight and must be stated separately. First, there are
mathematical structural theorems that do not depend materially on specifically EDC
physical postulates once the formal geometric structure is specified. These are the
strongest layer of the program. Second, there are physical structural derivations
that follow from explicitly adopted EDC postulates and model assumptions, such as
compactification choices, brane/bulk structure, linearization regimes, or specific
topological identifications. These are also genuine derivations, but they remain
conditional on acceptance of the underlying EDC postulates.

By contrast, current claims about fundamental numerical constants and dimensionful
parameters generally do not yet achieve full derivational closure. EDC currently
shows more strength in deriving or constraining the form of relations than in
uniquely deriving the values of constants. In this sense, the framework already
exhibits substantial structural closure, but only partial or still-open parameter
closure and observable closure. Parameter closure means determining the fundamental
5D parameters (σ, Rξ, and related quantities) uniquely from the action without
empirical input. Observable closure means deriving measured 3D constants as unique
numerical consequences of that closed parameter set. At present, many of the
strongest numerical outputs remain best classified as identified or calibrated,
not uniquely derived from first principles.

This distinction is not a weakness to be hidden but a defining feature of the
program's present epistemic honesty. Impressive numerical matches remain valuable
as clues, compression schemes, and heuristic indicators, but they are not
equivalent to first-principles derivation unless the relevant parameter and
observable closure has actually been achieved. Conversion from identified or
calibrated status to genuinely derived status requires, at minimum, parameter
closure: showing that the fundamental 5D parameters emerge uniquely from the
action rather than being calibrated against observed 3D values. The correct
present characterization is therefore this: EDC is currently stronger as a
structural-topological framework than as a closed theory of numerical fundamental
constants. The ongoing research program is to reduce that gap progressively and
explicitly — not by rhetorical inflation, but by converting structurally motivated
relations into genuinely derived ones wherever possible.

## Short Form (for reader contracts and paper introductions)

EDC is currently strongest at the level of structural closure: deriving symmetries,
topological constraints, and the allowed forms of physical interactions from 5D
geometry. Parameter closure — determining the fundamental 5D parameters (σ, Rξ,
and related quantities) uniquely from the action without empirical input — and
observable closure — deriving measured 3D constants as unique numerical
consequences — remain open research frontiers. Numerical matches presented in
this work are therefore best understood as identified or calibrated results unless
parameter and observable closure has been explicitly demonstrated.

## One-Line Form (for companion papers and section headers)

EDC currently achieves structural closure for symmetries and interaction forms,
while parameter and observable closure for fundamental constants remains an
open program.
