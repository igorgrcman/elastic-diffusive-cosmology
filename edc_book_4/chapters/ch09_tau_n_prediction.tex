\begin{abstract}
This chapter assembles the derivation chain: from the instanton formula
(Chapter~\ref{ch:instanton}), the topological factor $\kappa = 2\pi$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:kappa}), and the geometric ratio $\Lzero/\bdelta \approx \pi^2$
(Chapter~\ref{ch:L0delta}), we compute the metastable junction lifetime
$\taun \approx 880\second$. The result contains one postulated component
($\Lzero/\bdelta = \pi^2$~[P]) and one calibrated prefactor ($A \approx 0.9$~[Cal]).
The $< 1\%$ agreement with measured $\taun$ depends on both [P] and [Cal]
choices; a model-dependent eigenvalue analysis (Appendix~\ref{app:L0delta_bvp})
shows $\pi^2$ is one candidate among a continuous family, and the close agreement
is not independently confirmed without calibration.
\end{abstract}

\section{The Prefactor Block}
\label{sec:taun:prefactor}

\subsection{Physical Interpretation (EDC-Native)}

In the instanton framework, the prefactor represents the \emph{local mode scale}---the
rate at which the metastable junction ``attempts'' to tunnel through the barrier.

\textbf{EDC interpretation:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The junction oscillates in its local potential well at frequency $\omegaz$
    \item Each oscillation is an ``attempt'' to escape
    \item The escape probability per attempt is $\exp(-\SE/\hbar)$
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Attempt Frequency $\omegaz$}

The natural frequency is set by brane parameters:

\begin{derivationbox}[title=Attempt Frequency]
\begin{equation}
    \omegaz = \sqrt{\frac{\bsigma}{m_p}}
    \label{eq:omega0}
    \tagP
\end{equation}

With $\bsigma \approx 8.82\MeV/\fm^2$ and $m_p = 938.3\MeV$:
\begin{equation}
    \omegaz = \sqrt{\frac{8.82}{938.3}} \times c/\fm \approx 19\MeV
\end{equation}
\end{derivationbox}

The corresponding timescale:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\hbar}{\omegaz} = \frac{197.3\MeV\cdot\fm}{19\MeV \times c}
                         \approx 3.4 \times 10^{-23}\second
    \label{eq:hbar_omega}
\end{equation}

This is the characteristic timescale of junction dynamics---the ``junction''
timescale in EDC language.

\section{Anti-Tuning Firewall}
\label{sec:taun:antituning}

Before presenting the final numerical result, we establish the \emph{anti-tuning
firewall}---explicit documentation of what is locked versus what remains
calibrated.

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=gray!5,colframe=gray!50!black,title=Exponent vs.\ Prefactor Dominance]
The lifetime formula has the structure $\taun = A \cdot \tau_0 \cdot \exp(\SE/\hbar)$.
In log-space:
\[
    \log_{10}(\taun) = \log_{10}(A) + \log_{10}(\tau_0) + \frac{\SE/\hbar}{\ln 10}
\]

With $\SE/\hbar \approx 62$, the exponential contributes $\sim 27$ orders of magnitude.
The prefactor $A \sim 0.9$ contributes $< 0.1$ dex. Therefore:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{exponent dominates} the prediction by $> 99\%$ in log-space.
    \item The prefactor $A$ has \textbf{negligible influence} on the final value
          as long as $A = O(1)$.
    \item Varying $A$ by a factor of 3 changes $\taun$ by $\pm 0.5$ dex;
          varying the exponent by 5\% changes $\taun$ by $\pm 1$ dex.
\end{itemize}
\end{tcolorbox}

\textbf{Quarantine declaration:} The prefactor $A \approx 0.9$ is marked \tagCal{}
and quarantined in Appendix~\ref{app:quarantine}. The exponent
$\SE/\hbar = 2\pi^3$ uses the candidate value $\Lzero/\bdelta = \pi^2$~[P]
from Chapter~\ref{ch:L0delta}. A model-dependent eigenvalue analysis
(Appendix~\ref{app:L0delta_bvp}) shows that $\Lzero/\bdelta = F(\eta)$ is a
continuous function of well strength $\eta$~[P]; $\pi^2$ corresponds to
$\eta \approx 0.052$ and is not uniquely selected by the model. With the
semiclassical prefactor $A_{\text{sc}} \approx 0.84$~[Der within 1D model],
$\pi^2$ gives $\taun \approx 24{,}000\second$ --- the $< 1\%$ match with
observation requires either a different $\Lzero/\bdelta$ or treating $A$ as
calibrated~[Cal]. Sensitivity analysis in \S\ref{sec:taun:sensitivity}
demonstrates how $\taun$ varies across both $\Lzero/\bdelta$ and $A$.
