# Cover Letter — Physical Review C Submission

---

Dear Editors,

I am writing to submit the manuscript entitled **"Topological pinning predictions for α-decay half-lives of superheavy elements Z=119 and Z=120"** for consideration as a Regular Article in Physical Review C.

## Summary

This paper introduces a frustration-corrected Geiger–Nuttall law that adds a single physics-motivated correction term to the standard GN formula. The correction arises from a topological pinning model that predicts nuclei whose effective coordination number falls between elements of the allowed set S = {2^a × 3^b} experience enhanced α-decay tunneling.

## Key Results

- **Calibration:** The model is fitted to 106 known α-emitters (Z = 83–100), achieving R² = 0.980 with cross-validated R²_CV = 0.971.
- **Out-of-sample validation:** Six measured superheavy isotopes (Z = 114–118, withheld from training) yield mean absolute error of 0.48 dex—a 16× improvement over the uncorrected baseline. All six pass the |Δ| < 1.5 dex criterion.
- **Predictions:** Three undiscovered isotopes:
  - ²⁹⁸119: t₁/₂ ≈ 0.6 s (±1.3 dex)
  - ³⁰²120: t₁/₂ ≈ 30 s (±1.3 dex)
  - ³⁰⁴120 (N=184): t₁/₂ ≈ 780 s (±1.3 dex)

## Significance

Elements 119 and 120 are actively being pursued at JINR Dubna, GSI Darmstadt, and RIKEN. The predicted half-lives fall within detector capabilities at all three facilities. The ³⁰⁴120 prediction provides a direct test of the N = 184 spherical shell closure.

The frustration correction provides a qualitatively new explanation for why the standard Geiger–Nuttall law systematically overestimates superheavy half-lives by ~7 orders of magnitude: all superheavy nuclei fall in a "forbidden zone" of the coordination lattice where no element of the allowed set exists between 36 and 48.

## Scope

The paper is self-contained for nuclear physics readers. The underlying theoretical framework (elastic diffusive cosmology) is described at a level sufficient to motivate the allowed set S; the model is testable independently of the broader framework. Only one new parameter (the frustration coupling g) is introduced beyond the standard GN law.
