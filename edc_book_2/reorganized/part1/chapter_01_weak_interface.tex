\subsection{The Puzzle: Why 15 Minutes?}

The neutron lifetime $\tau_n = 879.4 \pm 0.6$ s \tagBL{} is bizarre:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]

\item Electromagnetic transitions take $10^{-16}$ to $10^{-8}$ s
\item Yet free neutron decay takes $\sim 10^3$ s---40 orders of magnitude slower!

The Standard Model explains this via the small Fermi coupling $G_F$. But \textit{why}

\subsection{Physical Picture (5D)}

\end{itemize}

The neutron is the \textit{same topological object} as the proton, just in an
excited configuration. Decay is geometric relaxation, not a point-particle vertex.

\subsection{WKB Estimate}

The barrier penetration gives a tunneling rate:
\begin{equation}
\Gamma \sim \omega_0 \exp\left(-\frac{2}{\hbar}\int_{q_1}^{q_2} \sqrt{2\mu(V(q) - E)} \, dq\right)
\equiv \omega_0 \, e^{-S/\hbar}
\label{eq:wkb_rate}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Variable dictionary}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $q$: Collective coordinate measuring junction asymmetry (dimensionless angle or
      normalized displacement); $q = 0$ = symmetric proton, $q = q_n$ = neutron configuration
\item $V(q)$: Effective potential for junction configuration (MeV); barrier between $n$ and $p$
\item $\mu$: Effective inertia for junction rearrangement (set by string tension and length scales)
\item $\omega_0$: Attempt frequency; if $\omega_0 \sim \Delta E/\hbar$ with $\Delta E \sim$ MeV,
      then $\omega_0 \sim 10^{21}$ s$^{-1}$
\item $S$: Barrier action (dimensionless in natural units $\hbar = 1$)
\end{itemize}

\quad\Rightarrow\quad
e^{-S} \sim 10^{-24}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
S \sim 55

\section{The Thick Brane Necessity}
\label{sec:thick_brane}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Why do weak interactions require a thick brane? This section explains the physical
necessity of finite brane thickness for weak processes.

\subsection{Thin Brane vs Thick Brane}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Thin brane} ($\delta \to 0$): Mathematical idealization. Fields strictly
      confined to a 4D hypersurface. Works for electromagnetic and strong interactions

      where particles are fully brane-localized.

\item \textbf{Thick brane} ($\delta > 0$): Physical model with finite thickness
      in the extra dimension. The brane has two distinct interfaces:
      \begin{enumerate}[nosep]
      \item \textbf{Bulk-facing side}: Where 5D bulk fields couple to brane dynamics
      \item \textbf{Observer-facing side}: Where effective 3D/4D physics emerges
      \end{enumerate}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why Weak Processes Need Thickness}

\textbf{The problem with thin branes}:

In a thin-brane limit, there is no room for:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Energy transfer from bulk structures (junctions) to brane modes
\item Intermediate states that mediate the transition
\item The ``two-face'' structure that distinguishes input from output
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What thick brane provides}:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Regularization}: Avoids singular delta-function sources in 5D equations

\item \textbf{Two-face structure}: Distinguishes where energy enters (bulk-facing)
      from where particles emerge (observer-facing)

\item \textbf{Mode localization}: Brane-layer modes have finite transverse extent,
      enabling selection rules

\item \textbf{Frozen boundary}: The one-way valve (energy flows in, particles come out)
      becomes a property of the observer-facing interface
\end{enumerate}

\begin{mechanism}{The Brane as a ``Processing Layer''}
Think of the thick brane as a processor between two worlds:

\textbf{Input side} (bulk-facing):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Receives energy from bulk dynamics (junction relaxation)
\item Couples to 5D fields
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Processing layer} (interior):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Redistributes energy into brane-layer modes
\item Applies selection rules
\item Determines allowed output channels
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Output side} (observer-facing):
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Projects allowed modes as 3D particles
\item What detectors measure
\item ``Frozen'' boundary condition
\end{itemize}
\end{mechanism}

\subsection{The Thickness Scale}

\textbf{What sets the brane thickness $\delta$?}

From membrane physics, the characteristic scale is:
\begin{equation}
\delta \sim \frac{\hbar c}{M_W c^2} \sim \frac{197 \text{ MeV}\cdot\text{fm}}{80 \text{ GeV}}
\sim 0.0025 \text{ fm} \quad \text{(weak scale)}
\end{equation}

This is much smaller than hadronic scales ($\sim 1$ fm) but finite. The smallness
of $\delta$ relative to hadronic scales is part of why weak interactions appear
``weak''---the bulk-to-brane transfer is geometrically suppressed.

\textbf{Frozen boundary (preview)}:
The observer-facing side of the brane has a ``frozen'' boundary condition:
outgoing bulk flux is absorbed, not reflected. Mathematically, this is a
Robin-type condition; physically, it implements the one-way valve
(energy in $\to$ particles out). Full treatment: Chapter~\ref{ch:frozen}.

\textbf{Connection to $G_F$}:

The Fermi coupling $G_F \sim 1/v^2$ where $v \approx 246$ GeV is the electroweak
scale. In EDC, this emerges from the brane thickness and the coupling between
bulk and brane modes.

\section{General Framework: Bulk $\to$ Brane Transfer}
\label{sec:general_framework}
% ════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

With the neutron example and thick-brane picture in mind, we now state the
general framework for weak interactions in EDC.

\subsection{The Unified Pipeline}

All weak processes in EDC follow a three-stage pipeline:

\begin{equation}
\text{Bulk dynamics} \;\xrightarrow{\;\Pi_{\mathrm{pump}}\;}\;
\text{Brane charging} \;\xrightarrow{\;\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}\;}\;
\text{3D output}
\label{eq:unified_pipeline}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Notation}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\Pi_{\mathrm{pump}}$: Energy flux from bulk to brane (dimension: power = energy/time)
\item $\Gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$: Effective decay rate for brane$\to$output projection (dimension: 1/time)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Stage 1: Bulk dynamics}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item 5D structures (junctions, defects) evolve in the bulk
\item Configuration changes release energy
\item Energy flows toward the brane
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Stage 2: Brane charging}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Brane absorbs energy from bulk dynamics
\item Energy redistributes into brane-layer modes
\item Selection rules determine allowed channels
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Stage 3: 3D output}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Brane modes project onto observer-facing side
\item Allowed particles emerge (electrons, neutrinos, etc.)
\item What detectors measure
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Why ``Weakness'' Is Geometric}

In this framework, the ``weakness'' of weak interactions has a clear geometric origin:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Bulk-to-brane transfer suppression}: Energy must cross from 5D bulk
      to 4D brane---this is geometrically suppressed

\item \textbf{Brane thickness}: The finite thickness $\delta$ sets the coupling
      scale between bulk and brane modes

\item \textbf{Selection rules}: Not all modes can exit---the frozen boundary
      condition filters allowed outputs
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Contrast with SM picture}:

In SM, weakness comes from a small coupling constant $g_W$ and heavy mediators
$M_W, M_Z$. In EDC, these are \textit{derived} from geometry:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $g_W$ reflects bulk-brane coupling strength
\item $M_W, M_Z$ reflect brane-layer eigenvalue spectrum
\item $G_F$ emerges from the combination
\end{itemize}
