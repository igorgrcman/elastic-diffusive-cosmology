\subsection{Observed Values}

Flavor eigenstates relate to mass eigenstates via the PMNS matrix \tagBL{}:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{pmatrix} \nu_e \\ \nu_\mu \\ \nu_\tau \end{pmatrix}
    = U_{\text{PMNS}}
    \begin{pmatrix} \nu_1 \\ \nu_2 \\ \nu_3 \end{pmatrix}

\begin{align}
    \sin^2\theta_{12} &\approx 0.307 \quad (\text{solar}) \\
    \sin^2\theta_{23} &\approx 0.546 \quad (\text{atmospheric}) \\
    \sin^2\theta_{13} &\approx 0.022 \quad (\text{reactor})
\end{align}

\subsection{EDC Interpretation (Postulated)}

The proposal is that PMNS mixing arises from overlap integrals between

\begin{equation}
    (U_{\text{PMNS}})_{\alpha i} \propto \int \psi^*_{\nu_\alpha}(\xi, \phi) \,

\subsection{Why Large Angles? CKM vs PMNS}

\textbf{Qualitative explanation} \tagI{}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item Neutrinos are \textbf{delocalized edge modes} (large $\kappa^{-1}$, spread over interface)
\item Quarks are \textbf{localized interior modes} (small $\kappa^{-1}$, tightly bound)
\item Larger delocalization $\to$ larger wavefunction overlaps $\to$ larger mixing angles
\end{itemize}
This explains why PMNS angles ($\sim 30$--$45^\circ$) are much larger than CKM angles
($\sim 13^\circ$, $2^\circ$, $0.2^\circ$).

\subsection{DFT Baseline (Falsified)}

If flavor and mass eigenstates have perfect $\mathbb{Z}_3$ symmetry, the PMNS
matrix would be the discrete Fourier transform:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

This predicts $\sin^2\theta_{13} = 1/3 \approx 0.33$, which is \textbf{15 times
larger} than the observed $0.022$ \tagBL{}.

\textbf{Conclusion:} The DFT baseline is \textbf{falsified}. $\mathbb{Z}_3$
symmetry must be broken at $\sim 25\%$ level in the $\nu_e$--$\nu_3$ sector.
