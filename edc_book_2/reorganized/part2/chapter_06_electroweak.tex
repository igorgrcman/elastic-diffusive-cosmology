The Weinberg angle parameterizes electroweak mixing:
\begin{equation}
\sin^2\theta_W = \frac{g'^2}{g^2 + g'^2}
\label{eq:weinberg_def}
\end{equation}
where $g$ and $g'$ are the $SU(2)_L$ and $U(1)_Y$ couplings.

In EDC, these couplings emerge from the $Z_6$ crystallographic symmetry of the
brane structure (Part I, Chapter 7). The key insight:

\begin{mechanism}{Weinberg Angle from Brane Geometry}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The brane has $Z_6$ symmetry from the hexagonal junction lattice
\item Gauge fields couple to different geometric sectors
\item $SU(2)_L$ couples to ``interior'' modes; $U(1)_Y$ to ``boundary'' modes
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Geometric calculation:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item The ratio $g'^2/g^2$ is determined by the relative ``volumes''
\item For $Z_6$: boundary/interior ratio $= 1/3$
\item Therefore: $\sin^2\theta_W = 1/(1+3) = 1/4$

\subsection{Physical Picture}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=green!5!white, colframe=green!50!black,
    title=\textbf{Physical Process: Membrane Thickness to Weak Scale}]

\textbf{Step 1: The brane is not infinitely thin.}
Our 3D universe is a membrane with finite thickness $\delta$. This arises from
the balance between bulk pressure and membrane tension \tagP{}.

\textbf{Step 2: Particles ``feel'' the boundaries.}
A 4D particle corresponds to a 5D mode $f(\xi)$ localized within the brane. The
mode must satisfy boundary conditions at the brane edges \tagDc{}.

\textbf{Step 3: Junction physics determines BC type.}
Israel junction conditions relate metric discontinuity to stress-energy. For
matter fields, this gives Robin-type BC: $f' + \alpha f = 0$ \tagDc{}.

\textbf{Step 4: The eigenvalue determines mediator mass.}
The BVP eigenvalue equation gives $m_\phi = x_1/\ell$, where $x_1$ depends
on $\alpha$. Different BCs give different spectra \tagDc{}.

\textbf{5D$\to$3D Pipeline:}
\[
\text{Thickness } \delta \;\to\; \alpha = \ell/\delta \;\to\;
x_1(\alpha) \;\to\; m_\phi = x_1/\ell \;\to\; G_F
\]
\end{tcolorbox}

\subsection{Robin Boundary Conditions}

The key insight is that boundary conditions are not a discrete choice (Dirichlet
vs Neumann) but a continuous family parameterized by $\alpha$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{BC type} & \textbf{Condition} & \textbf{Ground state $x_1$} \\
\midrule
Dirichlet & $f(0) = 0$ & $x_1 = \pi$ \\
Neumann & $f'(0) = 0$ & $x_1 = 0$ \\
Robin & $f'(0) + \alpha f(0) = 0$ & $x_1 \in (0, \pi)$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The eigenvalue $x_1$ interpolates continuously between the Dirichlet and Neumann
limits as $\alpha$ varies. This is standard Sturm-Liouville theory \tagM{}.

\subsection{The $\delta = R_\xi$ Identification}

A candidate for the boundary-layer scale $\delta$:
\begin{equation}
R_\xi = \frac{\hbar c}{M_Z} \approx 2.16 \times 10^{-3} \text{ fm} \quad \tagBL{}
\label{eq:Rxi}
\end{equation}

\begin{tcolorbox}[colback=yellow!5!white, colframe=yellow!50!black,
    title=\textbf{Warning: Identification vs Derivation}]
The identification $\delta = R_\xi$ is \textbf{plausible} but \textbf{not derived}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $R_\xi$ is defined via $M_Z$, an electroweak observable \tagBL{}
\item Using $R_\xi$ as input and then ``deriving'' $m_\phi \sim M_Z$ is circular
\item True closure requires deriving $\delta$ from $(\sigma, r_e)$ without SM input

\textbf{Current status:} $\delta = R_\xi$ is assumption \tagP{}, not result.

\subsection{Mechanism Overview}

The $W$ and $Z$ masses emerge from the eigenvalue spectrum of the brane BVP:
\begin{equation}
m_\phi = \frac{x_1(\alpha)}{\ell}
\label{eq:mphi}
\end{equation}

With the identification $\delta = R_\xi$ and $\ell \sim r_e$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\alpha = \ell/\delta \sim r_e/R_\xi \sim 450$ (large, near Dirichlet)
\item $x_1 \sim 2$--$3$ (between Neumann and Dirichlet limits)
\item $m_\phi \sim x_1 \cdot M_Z \cdot (R_\xi/\ell)$ gives $\sim 50$--$80$ GeV range
\end{itemize}
