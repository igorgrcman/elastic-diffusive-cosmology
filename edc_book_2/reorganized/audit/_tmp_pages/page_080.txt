11.4      Interface Mass Profile and Localization
We have the equations for the profiles. Now we need the mass function m(ξ). This is where
EDC physics enters.

11.4.1     The Domain Wall Mechanism (Baseline)
First, let us recall what is already known from standard physics [BL].
    The Jackiw–Rebbi–Kaplan mechanism. The localization of chiral fermions at domain
walls is a well-known result [1, 2]:
    • If m(ξ) increases from negative to positive values (e.g., m(ξ) = m0 tanh(z/L)), then the
      left-handed zero mode is localized at ξ = 0.
    • If m(ξ) decreases from positive to negative, the right-handed mode is localized.
    The physics: The sign of the mass profile determines chirality selection. Whichever chirality
is “uphill” in the mass landscape gets pushed to the minimum.

11.4.2     EDC Postulate: Plenum Inflow Determines Mass Sign
Now we add the EDC ingredient. In EDC, the Plenum (5D energy fluid) flows toward the
observer boundary:

This is the fundamental EDC mechanism (see Framework v2.0, Remark 4.5) [P].
   Why does this matter? The inflow creates a directional asymmetry in the 5D geometry.
The bulk and the boundary are not equivalent—energy flows from one to the other.

     Physical Hypothesis [P]

     The idea: The fermion mass m(ξ) is induced by coupling to the Plenum stress tensor.
     Where the energy flow is stronger, the effective mass is larger.

     Physical interpretation: A fermion feels “heavier” where the Plenum flow exerts more
