11.3: 5D Dirac Field and Chiral Decomposition                                                 63


   The equation. The 5D Dirac equation with position-dependent mass is:

                             iγ µ ∂µ + iγ 5 ∂ξ − m(ξ) Ψ = 0                                (11.2)
                                                     


This is standard relativistic quantum mechanics extended to 5D (no [BL] tag—it is mathematical
structure, not empirical data). This is the standard Dirac equation, except that the mass is not
a constant—it depends on where you are in the fifth dimension.
    What each term does:
    • γ µ are the standard 4D Dirac matrices, {γ µ , γ ν } = 2η µν
    • γ 5 = iγ 0 γ 1 γ 2 γ 3 is the chirality operator
    • m(ξ) is the position-dependent fermion mass (to be determined by EDC physics)
    The key is the term iγ 5 ∂ξ : it couples left-handed and right-handed components differently
to the ξ-direction. This is where chirality enters.


11.3.2    Chiral Projection Operators

The definition. We split any spinor into left-handed and right-handed parts. Define the 4D
chiral projectors [M]:
                                1                     1
                          PL = (1 − γ 5 ),      PR = (1 + γ 5 )                      (11.3)
                                2                     2
These satisfy PL + PR = 1, PL PR = 0, and γ 5 PL/R = ∓PL/R .
   The decomposition. Any spinor splits cleanly into two pieces:

                      Ψ(x, ξ) = ΨL (x, ξ) + ΨR (x, ξ),    ΨL/R = PL/R Ψ                    (11.4)

Think of ΨL and ΨR as two species of particle that can transform into each other through the
mass term.


11.3.3    Mode Expansion

Zero-mode limit. The equations below correspond to the chiral zero-mode limit where
the 4D mass eigenvalue m4 = 0. For massive 4D modes (m4 ̸= 0), the left- and right-handed
profiles couple through the 4D mass term, leading to a second-order Schrödinger-like eigenvalue
problem (see the BVP work package, Chapter 15). The zero-mode analysis captures the leading
chirality-selection mechanism; massive-mode corrections are higher-order.

    Separating variables. We want to find how the left- and right-handed pieces are distributed
in ξ. For a massless 4D mode with momentum pµ , write:

                              Ψ(x, ξ) = ψL (x)fL (ξ) + ψR (x)fR (ξ)                        (11.5)

Here ψL/R (x) are 4D spinor fields and fL/R (ξ) are ξ-profiles. The profiles tell us: how much of
each chirality lives at each depth?
   The profile equations. Substituting into Eq. (11.2) and separating chiralities gives the
coupled first-order equations [Der]:

                                       ∂ξ fL = −m(ξ)fL                                     (11.6)
                                       ∂ξ fR = +m(ξ)fR                                     (11.7)

Notice the crucial sign difference: fL gets a minus sign, fR gets a plus sign.
    What this means physically: If m(ξ) > 0, then fL decreases with ξ (the left-handed mode
is pushed toward smaller ξ), while fR increases with ξ (the right-handed mode is pushed toward
larger ξ).
