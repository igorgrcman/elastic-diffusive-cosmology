2.2      Quick Win: Neutron Lifetime from Geometry
Let’s start with a concrete result to show the framework works.


2.2.1     The Puzzle: Why 15 Minutes?
The neutron lifetime τn = 879.4 ± 0.6 s [BL] is bizarre:
   • Strong interactions happen in 10−23 s
   • Electromagnetic transitions take 10−16 to 10−8 s
   • Yet free neutron decay takes ∼ 103 s—40 orders of magnitude slower!
   The Standard Model explains this via the small Fermi coupling GF . But why is GF so small?
EDC provides a geometric answer.


2.2.2     Physical Picture (5D)
From Part I, we established [Der]:
   • Proton: Y-junction at 120◦ angles (Steiner minimum)—stable ground state
   • Neutron: Asymmetric Y-junction (not at 120◦ )—excited state
   The neutron is the same topological object as the proton, just in an excited configuration.
Decay is geometric relaxation, not a point-particle vertex.

     5D Mechanism: Neutron Decay as Junction Relaxation

     In 5D:
         • Initial state: Asymmetric junction (neutron), displaced from Steiner minimum
         • Final state: Symmetric junction at 120◦ (proton)
         • Barrier: Geometric rearrangement through brane thickness
         The decay process:
        1. Junction configuration qn > 0 (excited) relaxes toward q = 0 (proton)
        2. Energy released: ∆mnp = 1.293 MeV [BL]
        3. This energy pumps into the brane layer
        4. Brane modes organize into allowed outputs: e− + ν̄e + p
         Key insight: The neutrino is NOT just another particle. It’s an edge mode—a
     boundary excitation that carries energy into the bulk. The electron emerges as a brane-
     localized spherical defect.
