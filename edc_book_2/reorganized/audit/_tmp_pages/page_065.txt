Chapter 9

Three Generations: Why Not Four?

      This chapter addresses one of the deepest puzzles in particle physics: why does the
      fermion spectrum consist of exactly three generations? EDC proposes a geometric
      answer rooted in Z6 crystallography.


9.1    The Generation Puzzle
   Epistemic Status: HIGH RISK

   What we have:
       • An identification linking Z3 ⊂ Z6 to generation count [I]
       • Mode indices n = 0, 1, 2 mapped to (e, µ, τ ) with < 1% mass error [I]
       • Multiple candidate mechanisms for truncation (none fully derived)
   What remains open:
       • A rigorous derivation of why exactly three modes survive
       • The dynamical mechanism forbidding n ≥ 3 modes
   Honest assessment: This chapter documents an identification, not a derivation. The
   structural pattern is compelling; the proof is incomplete.

   In the Standard Model, the existence of three fermion generations is an empirical input.
The gauge structure SU (3)C × SU (2)L × U (1)Y permits any number of generations; the value
Ngen = 3 is simply observed [BL].
   EDC aims to derive Ngen = 3 from geometric structure:
                                                          ?
                        Z6 = Z2 × Z3    =⇒     |Z3 | = 3 ←→ Ngen = 3                        (9.1)

   Dictionary: What carries Z3 charge?

   Open question [Open]: The physical object carrying Z3 index could be:
      • Angular sector of localized wavefunction ψ(ξ, ϕ) with ϕ ∈ {0, 2π/3, 4π/3}
      • Eigenmode index n ∈ {0, 1, 2} of a BVP tower
      • Topological winding class in the brane’s internal space
      • Superselection sector from boundary conditions
   Status: This is an identification of structure [I], not a derivation of generation count.
   The “=” in |Z3 | = Ngen is a matching, not a proof.

   The challenge is to make this connection rigorous, not merely suggestive.
