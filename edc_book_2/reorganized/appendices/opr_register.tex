\subsection*{OPR-28: G Formula Exponent Derivation}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\textbf{Status:} & \tagI{} (Identified by fitting; derivation not achieved) \\
\textbf{Location:} & Part~I gravity sector (cross-sector reference) \\
\textbf{Dependencies:} & 5D action, dimensional reduction framework \\
\textbf{Blocks:} & First-principles gravity-sector closure \\
\textbf{Current formula:} & $G = c^4 R_\xi^{12}/(128\pi^2 \sigma r_e^{13})$, ~0.81\% error \\
\end{tabular}

\textbf{Problem statement:} Derive the exponent structure (powers 12, 13)
and the prefactor $128\pi^2$ in the gravitational constant formula from
5D action / EDC first principles.

\textbf{Negative result:} Standard Kaluza--Klein 5D$\to$4D reduction yields
$G_4 = G_5/(2\pi R_\xi)$, which gives power $-1$ for $R_\xi$, not $+12$.
No known physical mechanism within the current 5D framework generates the
required power 12.

\textbf{Non-uniqueness:} Multiple exponent combinations $(n, m)$ satisfying
$n + m = -1$ can fit $G_{\mathrm{CODATA}}$ by adjusting the geometric prefactor.
The pair $(12, -13)$ is not uniquely fixed by the data.

\textbf{Epistemic status rationale:}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \tagDc{} is too strong: no derivation path is closed, not even conditionally.
\item \tagCal{} is too strong for the exponent \emph{form} itself: calibration
  presupposes a unique functional form, but the exponent pair is not unique.
\item \tagI{} is the safe current status: identified candidate form with
  numerical fit, but neither derived nor uniquely constrained.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Upgrade condition:} First-principles derivation from 5D EDC action
that uniquely produces powers 12 and 13 and the factor $128\pi^2$,
or a proof that these are the unique exponents consistent with
EDC postulates and dimensional constraints beyond $n + m = -1$.

\textbf{Source:} Documented negative result in \texttt{task\_b5\_power\_derivation.md}
(private repo, branch \texttt{research/neutron-proton-mass-difference-5D}).

\bigskip

\subsection*{OPR-33: $R_\xi$ Nomenclature Ambiguity}
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\textbf{Status:} & \tagResolved{} (Verdict~C: one value is superseded) \\
\textbf{Location:} & Cross-cutting: Paper~2 vs.\ Part~I/v21/v22 \\
\textbf{Dependencies:} & Part~I Ch~6 \S6.4 ``Important Correction'' \\
\textbf{Blocks:} & Symbol hygiene for all $R_\xi$-dependent formulae \\
\textbf{Audit document:} & \texttt{RXI\_AMBIGUITY\_AUDIT.md} \\
\end{tabular}

\textbf{Problem statement:} Two incompatible values appear under the same
symbol~$R_\xi$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{KK (v21):} $R_\xi = \pi\hbar c / M_Z \approx 6.80 \times 10^{-18}$~m
      (compactification radius from Kaluza--Klein mass gap $= M_Z$).
\item \textbf{Paper~2:} $R_\xi = 136\,r_e \approx 383$~fm (from $\alpha = r_e/(R_\xi + r_e)$).
\end{itemize}
The ratio is $\sim 56{,}000\times$ --- not a convention difference.

\textbf{Verdict:} Both claim to be the compactification radius (Hypothesis~A:
same quantity, contradiction). Part~I Ch~6 \S6.4 explicitly supersedes the old
model, establishing $R_\xi \sim 10^{-18}$~m and correcting the fine-structure
formula to $\alpha = m_e c^2 / (\sigma_{\text{eff}}\, r_e^2)$, which depends
on~$r_e$, \emph{not}~$R_\xi$ (Verdict~C: Paper~2 value is wrong/superseded).

\textbf{Key finding:} Paper~2's $136\,r_e \approx 383$~fm $\approx \lambda_C$
(Compton wavelength $= 386$~fm, ratio 1.008). Paper~2 mislabeled the Compton
wavelength as the compactification radius.

\textbf{Residual action:} Part~I Ch~0 line~1523 retains the superseded formula
$\alpha = m_e c^2 / (\sigma R_\xi^2)$ --- flagged as~F6 in the correction manifest.

\textbf{Source:} \texttt{edc\_book\_4/derivations/RXI\_AMBIGUITY\_AUDIT.md},
dated 2026-03-16.
