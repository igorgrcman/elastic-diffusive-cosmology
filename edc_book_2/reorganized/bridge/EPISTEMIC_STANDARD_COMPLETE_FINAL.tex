\section*{On Certainty, Verification, and Epistemic Status}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{On Certainty, Verification, and Epistemic Status}

\subsection*{Scope: What This Section Guarantees}
This section formalizes how claims in Book~2 are stated, verified, and tagged.
It is designed to make the derivational status of each statement explicit:
what is purely mathematical, what is postulated, and where the model connects to
3D observables (PDG/experimental baselines).

\subsection*{What the Model Yields (and What It Does Not)}
The EDC framework \textbf{yields} specific quantities \emph{within the model} from 5D geometry.
Some of these quantities \textbf{match current measurements} at the sub-percent level.

\textbf{Caveat (non-negotiable):} such agreement is \emph{conditional} on a reduction dictionary
(mapping 5D quantities to 3D observables) and on normalization conventions.
Therefore, agreement is treated as strong evidence, \textbf{not} absolute proof.

\subsection*{Sources of Uncertainty (Why 100\% Certainty Is Not Claimed)}
We do not claim absolute certainty because:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Reduction physics is incomplete} \tagOpen{}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Boundary conditions and domain choices may not be unique.
  \item Systematic correction factors may exist.
  \item Normalization conventions involve nontrivial choices.
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Higher-order effects are not fully computed} \tagOpen{}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Electromagnetic/self-energy corrections ($O(\alpha)$).
  \item Finite-size effects and compositeness corrections.
  \item Renormalization-group running and scheme dependence (where relevant).
  \end{itemize}

\item \textbf{Core postulates remain hypotheses} \tagP{}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item 5D membrane structure and brane thickness mechanism.
  \item $Z_6$ crystallographic ansatz.
  \item Specific topological defect identifications.
  \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Mathematical Precision Levels (Define ``Exact'')}
Whenever the text says \emph{exact}, it must be one of the following:

\paragraph{\textbf{Level 1: Exact Symbolic} \;\;[Der-Sym]}
Closed-form algebraic expressions, no truncation.
Example: $m_p/m_e = 6\pi^5$ \tagDer{}.

\paragraph{\textbf{Level 2: Exact Numerical} \;\;[Der-Num]}
Deterministic numerical procedure with controlled tolerance.
Example: a definite overlap integral evaluated to $\le 10^{-10}$ numerical tolerance.
Numerical stability checks must be stated (grid refinement / alternate solver).

\paragraph{\textbf{Level 3: Exact Within an Approximation} \;\;[Dc-Approx]}
Exact at the stated order/limit (e.g.\ tree-level, thin/thick-brane limit, leading order in $\alpha$).
Corrections are tracked as \tagOpen{} with estimated magnitude where possible.

\subsection*{Epistemic Tags (Formal Boundary Rules)}

\subsubsection*{[M] --- Mathematics}
Pure mathematical theorem, independent of physics; provable within a formal system. \tagM{}

\subsubsection*{[P] --- Postulate}
A starting physical hypothesis about the 5D structure. \tagP{}

\subsubsection*{[Der] --- Pure Derivation}
A mathematical consequence of \tagM{} and \tagP{} \emph{without claiming identification with a PDG observable}.
If the statement remains internal to the model, it stays \tagDer{}.

\subsubsection*{[Dc] --- Conditionally Derived (Default for Physics Results)}
A claim that \emph{connects} a model quantity to an external 3D observable.
This requires a dictionary (interpretation + units + normalization + reduction). \tagDc{}

\subsubsection*{Critical Boundary Rule (Non-negotiable)}
\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{
\textbf{Rule:} Any claim of the form
\[
X_{\mathrm{EDC}} \equiv Y_{\mathrm{PDG/obs}}
\]
is \tagDc{}, even if the internal derivation of $X_{\mathrm{EDC}}$ is fully \tagDer{}.
The identification step requires physical interpretation, units/normalization, and reduction mapping.
}}
\end{center}

\subsubsection*{[I] vs [Cal] --- Sharp Distinction}
\paragraph{\textbf{[I] Identified}}
Structural similarity recognition \emph{without} numerical parameter tuning:
``looks like Schwarzschild'', ``mode structure suggests $\cos(n\theta)$'', etc. \tagI{}

\paragraph{\textbf{[Cal] Calibrated}}
Any explicit adjustment of a free parameter to match data (even once):
``we set $A=0.84$ to fit $\tau_n$'' is \tagCal{}.

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.93\textwidth}{
\textbf{Simple rule:}
[I] = form recognition without tuning;\quad
[Cal] = parameter value chosen to match a measurement.
}}
\end{center}

\subsection*{Verification Standard: What Must Accompany Any ``Match to Data'' Claim}
For each major result that is compared to measurement, the text must include:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Derivation category}: [Der-Sym], [Der-Num], or [Dc-Approx].
\item \textbf{Dictionary statement}: what exactly is being identified with what observable.
\item \textbf{Error budget table}: at least order-of-magnitude estimates, and which terms are \tagOpen{}.
\item \textbf{Sensitivity note}: what changes under plausible variation of conventions/assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Epistemic Posture (Neutral, Non-defensive)}
This work prioritizes \textbf{transparency over certainty claims}:
strong agreement is reported where warranted, incompleteness is explicitly bounded,
and falsification criteria are stated. All major claims carry epistemic tags
(\tagM{}, \tagP{}, \tagDer{}, \tagDc{}, \tagI{}, \tagCal{}, \tagBL{}, \tagOpen{})
that expose their conditional dependencies.
