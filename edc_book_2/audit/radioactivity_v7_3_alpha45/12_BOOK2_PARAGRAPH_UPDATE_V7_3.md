## Context

The EDC framework predicts that the coordination distance d(n) — measuring how far a nucleus's effective coordination number n(A) falls from the nearest allowed M-topology value — should influence decay rates. Nuclei with larger d(n) experience greater "frustration" and should decay faster toward lower-d(n) products.

## Suggested Paragraph (Conservative)

> **Coordination Effects in Alpha Decay** [P]
>
> The M-topology coordination law (n = 2^a × 3^b) creates a "forbidden zone" between n = 37 and n = 47, where no integer is expressible in this form. Heavy nuclei with n(A) in this range experience coordination frustration quantified by d(n) = min|n(A) - m| for allowed m. An analysis of 45 α-emitters with complete spin-parity data shows a suggestive negative correlation (r ≈ -0.28, p ≈ 0.05) between d(n) and Geiger-Nuttall residuals after controlling for hindrance class, consistent with the prediction that higher frustration accelerates decay. The three canonical decay chains (U-238, Th-232, U-235) all terminate at ²⁰⁸Pb, the nearest doubly-magic nucleus to n = 36. However, statistical power remains limited (n = 45, power ≈ 52%), and the effect should be classified as suggestive rather than confirmed. Nuclear structure effects (spin-parity selection rules) dominate over any coordination preference at branchpoints, consistent with the hierarchy where topology modulates rates within allowed channels but does not override angular momentum conservation.
