## Variant 1: Minimal (Safest)

> Regression analysis of 102 α-emitting nuclides reveals a statistically significant correlation between the M-topology coordination distance d(n) and decay rate, beyond what is explained by the Geiger-Nuttall law and hindrance factors. The coefficient g = −0.31 ± 0.11 (p = 0.006) indicates that nuclei with coordination indices farther from allowed values decay *faster*, not slower. This finding survives cross-validation, permutation testing, and control for parity classes. The sign suggests that topological "frustration" enhances rather than impedes decay, possibly through preformation dynamics rather than barrier modification.

## Variant 2: With Physical Interpretation

> Among 102 α-emitters from Po to Fm, the coordination distance d(n) shows a robust negative correlation with half-life: each unit increase in d(n) corresponds to roughly a factor of two faster decay. Model comparison favors a prefactor interpretation over barrier modification — that is, d(n) appears to enhance the α-preformation probability S_α rather than reduce tunneling probability. This is consistent with a "frustration dynamics" picture: nuclei whose coordination deviates from allowed M-topology values experience enhanced surface reorganization, facilitating α-cluster formation. The effect is strongest in unhindered transitions (H0), where it is not masked by selection rules.

## Variant 3: Cautious with Alternatives

> The d(n) coefficient g = −0.31 (p = 0.006) indicates that α-decay is faster, not slower, for nuclei farther from allowed M-topology values. This contradicts the naive expectation that "forbidden" zones would impede decay. Three interpretations remain viable: (i) frustration enhances preformation probability S_α through surface dynamics; (ii) d(n) is a proxy for unmeasured nuclear structure (deformation, pairing residuals); (iii) the effect is statistical coincidence despite p = 0.006. The first interpretation is favored by model comparison (AIC) and the observation that the effect persists after controlling for parity classes, but causation is not established.
